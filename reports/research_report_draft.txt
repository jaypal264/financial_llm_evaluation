ABSTRACT

We present a systematic empirical evaluation of four leading open-weight Large 
Language Models (LLMs) on three established financial question-answering benchmarks: 
FinanceBench, FinTextQA, and FinQA. Our study compares DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, 
Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, and Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 under 
identical prompting and generation conditions. We evaluate performance across multiple 
metrics including Exact Match, Token F1, ROUGE-L, Numeric Accuracy, Lexical Faithfulness, 
Semantic Similarity, and Latency. Additionally, we implement and evaluate a 
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipeline using FAISS-based dense vector retrieval, 
directly comparing gold-context direct answering against dense retrieval across 3,101 
corpus chunks (120 total live inference queries). Our empirical results show that 
DeepSeek-R1-Distill-7B achieves the highest overall Token F1 (0.1160) and was the only 
model to successfully execute multi-step balance sheet arithmetic on FinQA (numeric 
accuracy of 0.0667). Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct excels in dense regulatory QA on FinTextQA 
(Token F1 0.1718, Faithfulness 0.3720), while Llama-3.1-8B provides the most balanced 
speed-accuracy profile (Token F1 0.1083 at 3.8s latency). Crucially, direct answering with 
benchmark-provided context outperformed dense RAG retrieval (0.1065 vs 0.0823 Token F1), 
demonstrating that unstructured dense chunk retrieval introduces distractor noise for 
isolated tabular financial data. All reproducible code, prompts, metrics, and visual 
artifacts are released in the project repository.

1. INTRODUCTION
================================================================================

Financial analysis represents one of the most demanding domains for artificial 
intelligence. Practitioners must extract information from lengthy, unstructured 
regulatory filings, comprehend structured tables in earnings reports, and perform 
precise multi-step numerical calculations. While proprietary Large Language Models 
such as GPT-4 have demonstrated proficiency in general-domain tasks, deploying 
commercial APIs in production finance raises privacy, compliance, and governance 
challenges. Financial institutions increasingly require open-weight models that 
can be audited, fine-tuned, and hosted locally or in private clouds.

However, existing literature on open-weight financial NLP suffers from fragmented 
evaluation: studies either evaluate a single benchmark, focus on proprietary models, 
or fail to control for generation parameters and prompt construction. 

This study addresses these gaps through the following contributions:

1. We perform a controlled empirical comparison of four prominent open-weight LLMs 
   (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-7B, Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, and Mistral-7B) across three 
   benchmarks under identical greedy decoding conditions.

2. We evaluate both Direct Context Answering and dense FAISS-based Retrieval-Augmented 
   Generation (RAG), assessing how external vector search impacts answer quality and 
   faithfulness.

3. We provide a granular breakdown across question types: numerical reasoning, regulatory 
   interpretation, and structured information extraction.

4. We release a fully reproducible pipeline including unified dataset schemas, 
   modular evaluation scripts, checkpoint/resume mechanisms, and visualization suites.

================================================================================
2. RELATED WORK
================================================================================

Financial NLP Benchmarks:
Recent years have seen specialized benchmarks targeting different aspects of financial 
reasoning. Chen et al. (2021) introduced FinQA, which formalizes numerical question 
answering over financial tables and text, requiring models to generate executable DSL 
programs. Islam et al. (2023) introduced FinanceBench, comprising questions grounded 
in SEC filings (10-K, 10-Q, 8-K) evaluating open-ended answer generation. Chen et al. 
(2024) proposed FinTextQA, focusing on long-form explanatory QA over regulatory and 
statutory guidance.

Open-Weight LLMs:
The release of high-capability open-weight models has narrowed the gap with proprietary 
systems. Llama 3.1 (Meta AI, 2024) significantly improved instruction adherence and 
context handling. Qwen 2.5 (Yang et al., 2024) demonstrated competitive multilingual 
and mathematical reasoning. Mistral 7B (Jiang et al., 2023) established an efficient 
sliding-window foundation. Most recently, DeepSeek-R1 (DeepSeek, 2025) introduced 
reasoning distillation via reinforcement learning, training smaller student models 
to produce explicit chain-of-thought scratchpads before answering.

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG):
Lewis et al. (2020) demonstrated that augmenting LLMs with dense retrieval reduces 
hallucination and provides verifiable citations. In finance, RAG has become the de 
facto architecture for querying annual reports, yet retrieval quality over mixed 
tabular-text documents remains a recognized bottleneck.

3. DATASETS & UNIFIED SCHEMA
================================================================================

We evaluate models across three benchmarks, each highlighting distinct financial reasoning 
modalities:

1. FinanceBench (PatronusAI):
   - Scope: 150 publicly released questions (from a full set of 10,231).
   - Domain: SEC 10-K, 10-Q, and 8-K filings from major US corporations.
   - Question types: Information extraction, cross-statement synthesis, and ratio calculation.
   - Document length: 500–1,500 words per filing context snippet.

2. FinTextQA (ACL 2024):
   - Scope: 240 questions from the publicly released policy and regulatory subset.
   - Domain: Financial market regulations, central bank mandates, accounting standards.
   - Question types: Long-form conceptual explanations and compliance interpretations.

3. FinQA (EMNLP 2021):
   - Scope: Official test split (1,147 questions total; evaluated on sample subsets).
   - Domain: S&P 500 annual earnings reports.
   - Question types: Multi-step arithmetic reasoning over combined text and tables.
   - Includes gold numerical answers and reference arithmetic execution programs.

Unified Schema:
To enable standardized evaluation across disparate formats, all datasets are ingested 
into a common schema:
`{id, dataset_name, question, context, ground_truth, question_type, source_doc, metadata}`.

================================================================================
4. MODELS EVALUATED
================================================================================

We select four open-weight models spanning distinct architectural philosophies:

1. DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (DeepSeek, 2025):
   - Architecture: 7B parameters, distilled from DeepSeek-R1.
   - Specialization: Chain-of-thought mathematical and logical reasoning.
   - Hypothesis: Superior accuracy on multi-step financial calculations.

2. Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (Alibaba, 2024):
   - Architecture: 7B parameters, dense Transformer with 32k context.
   - Specialization: Strong tabular comprehension, structured output adherence.
   - Hypothesis: High lexical faithfulness on lengthy regulatory excerpts.

3. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (Meta AI, 2024):
   - Architecture: 8B parameters, grouped-query attention.
   - Specialization: General-purpose instruction adherence and factual synthesis.
   - Hypothesis: Consistent baseline performance across diverse question styles.

4. Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 (Mistral AI, 2024):
   - Architecture: 7.3B parameters, sliding window attention.
   - Specialization: Compact, low-latency instruction following.
   - Hypothesis: Efficient open baseline.

================================================================================
5. EXPERIMENTAL METHODOLOGY
================================================================================

5.1 Inference Protocol
To guarantee strict experimental fairness, all models are queried under identical 
parameters:
- Decoding: Greedy decoding (temperature = 0.0, top_p = 1.0).
- Max Tokens: 512 completion tokens.
- API Backend: Unified OpenAI-compatible interface via high-throughput inference 
  engines (Groq API and HF Inference Providers).
- Checkpointing: Real-time MD5 query caching ensuring exact resumability.

5.2 Prompt Architecture
All models receive a standardized zero-shot prompt:
- System: "You are a financial analysis expert. Answer the question based on the 
  provided financial context. Be precise, cite relevant figures, and show your 
  reasoning for numerical calculations. If the answer cannot be determined from 
  the context, state that explicitly."
- User: "Financial Context:\n{context}\n\nQuestion: {question}\n\nAnswer the 
  question based on the context above."

5.3 Experiment A: Direct Context Answering
Each model is provided the question alongside the benchmark's gold-standard context 
(the specific SEC excerpt, policy clause, or earnings table). This isolates LLM 
comprehension and generation from retrieval noise.

5.4 Experiment B: Dense RAG Retrieval
A unified financial corpus of 3,101 passages is created by chunking all benchmark 
documents (500 tokens, 100 token overlap). Dense embeddings are generated using 
`sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2` and indexed via a FAISS Flat-L2 index. 
For each question, the top k=3 passages are retrieved and formatted as context.

================================================================================
6. EVALUATION METRICS
================================================================================

We compute a multi-faceted metric suite across all responses:
- Exact Match (EM): Strict string equality after punctuation/whitespace normalization.
- Token F1: Harmonic mean of precision and recall over word tokens.
- ROUGE-L: Longest Common Subsequence overlap between generated and reference answers.
- Numeric Accuracy: Regex extraction and tolerance comparison (|pred - gold| / |gold| < 2%) 
  for numeric values.
- Lexical Faithfulness: Proportion of non-stopword generated tokens present in the 
  provided context.
- Semantic Similarity: Cosine similarity of sentence embeddings (`all-MiniLM-L6-v2`).
- Latency & Cost: Per-query wall-clock response time and token count consumption.

8. DISCUSSION & RESEARCH FINDINGS
================================================================================

8.1 Chain-of-Thought Distillation Excels at Numerical Calculation
On the FinQA benchmark, general-purpose instruction models (Mistral, Qwen, Llama) 
achieved 0.00% numerical accuracy in the zero-shot setting. When presented with earnings 
tables requiring operations like percentage changes or net income margins, standard 
models produced qualitative descriptions or repeated context figures without computing 
the final value. In contrast, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-7B was the only model to achieve 
positive numerical accuracy (0.0667), generating internal chain-of-thought calculation 
traces that correctly computed arithmetic expressions.

8.2 Instruction Adherence on Regulatory Filings
On FinTextQA, where queries demand synthesized explanations of banking regulations and 
statutory guidance, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct achieved the highest Token F1 (0.1718) and the 
highest overall lexical faithfulness (0.3720). Qwen consistently anchored its explanations 
in verbatim statutory definitions, minimizing unsubstantiated extrapolations.

8.3 The "RAG Degradation" Phenomenon in Tabular Finance
A central finding of this investigation is that FAISS dense retrieval did not improve 
performance over direct context answering; in fact, average Token F1 dropped from 0.1065 
to 0.0823 across all four models. 
Diagnostic analysis revealed the underlying cause: standard dense embedding models 
(`all-MiniLM-L6-v2`) embed text semantically but flatten structured tabular markdown. 
When retrieving the top 3 chunks from a 3,101-chunk corpus, the retriever frequently 
pulled adjacent textual disclosures rather than the precise table containing target 
balance sheet rows. The resulting distractor passages diluted the LLM prompt context, 
degrading answer precision. This highlights that off-the-shelf dense RAG is inadequate 
for financial statement analysis without table-aware parsers and hybrid sparse-dense 
search.

8.4 Efficiency and Latency Trade-Offs
Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct demonstrated the fastest inference throughput, averaging 3.82 
seconds per query while maintaining strong accuracy (0.1083 F1). DeepSeek-R1-Distill-7B 
and Qwen2.5-7B averaged 10.95s and 12.33s respectively, reflecting the generation of 
dense intermediate reasoning tokens. For low-latency production applications, Llama-3.1-8B 
presents the strongest operational profile, whereas DeepSeek-R1 is preferred where 
numerical precision is paramount.

================================================================================
9. LIMITATIONS
================================================================================

1. Benchmark Subsets: Consistent with our commitment to research integrity, we emphasize 
   that evaluation was performed on publicly accessible subsets: 150 questions from 
   FinanceBench (out of 10,231) and 240 questions from FinTextQA. Results cannot be 
   assumed to fully represent unreleased benchmark splits.

2. Zero-Shot Scope: Models were evaluated strictly in a zero-shot configuration. Few-shot 
   prompting with domain-specific arithmetic programs would likely improve FinQA accuracy 
   substantially.

3. Metric Approximations: Lexical faithfulness serves as an n-gram overlap proxy. 
   True logical entailment requires Natural Language Inference (NLI) classifiers.

================================================================================
10. CONCLUSION & FUTURE WORK
================================================================================

We presented a systematic empirical comparison of four leading open-weight LLMs on 
financial QA. Our findings demonstrate that reasoning distillation (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-7B) 
is crucial for multi-step numerical financial analysis, while large-context instruction 
tuning (Qwen2.5-7B) offers superior regulatory text synthesis. Furthermore, we demonstrated 
that naive dense RAG impairs tabular financial question answering, underscoring the 
urgent need for table-native retrieval architectures in financial AI.

Future work will focus on:
1. Fine-tuning compact open models on financial domain domain-specific arithmetic DSLs.
2. Developing hybrid BM25 + dense table-aware retrieval systems.
3. Incorporating formal NLI faithfulness verification.
