ABSTRACT

We present a systematic empirical evaluation of six open-weight Large Language 
Models (LLMs) on three established financial question-answering benchmarks: 
FinanceBench, FinTextQA, and FinQA. Our study compares Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, 
Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, Gemma-2-9B-it, 
Phi-3.5-mini-instruct, and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B under identical prompting 
and generation conditions using the Hugging Face Inference Providers API. We 
evaluate performance across multiple metrics including Exact Match, Token F1, 
ROUGE-L, Numeric Accuracy, and faithfulness proxies. Additionally, we implement 
and evaluate a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipeline using FAISS-based 
dense retrieval, comparing direct LLM answering against RAG-augmented inference.

release all evaluation code, prompts, and results to support reproducible research 
in financial NLP.

1. INTRODUCTION
================================================================================

Large Language Models have demonstrated impressive performance across general 
natural language understanding and generation tasks (Brown et al., 2020; 
Touvron et al., 2023; Jiang et al., 2023). However, the financial domain presents 
unique challenges that go beyond general language understanding. Financial question 
answering requires: (1) precise numerical reasoning over tabular data from earnings 
reports and balance sheets; (2) faithful extraction of specific figures from dense 
financial documents such as SEC 10-K and 10-Q filings; (3) understanding of 
specialized financial concepts and regulatory language; and (4) resistance to 
hallucination, which is particularly costly in financial decision-making contexts.

Despite growing interest in financial NLP, there remain significant gaps in our 
understanding of how open-weight LLMs — models whose weights are publicly 
available — compare on specialized financial benchmarks. Most existing studies 
either focus on proprietary models (e.g., GPT-4), evaluate a single benchmark, 
or do not control for inference conditions across models. This makes fair 
comparison difficult.

This paper addresses this gap with the following contributions:

1. We perform a controlled comparison of six diverse open-weight LLMs across 
   three financial QA benchmarks under identical prompting and generation conditions.

2. We implement and evaluate a RAG pipeline, comparing direct LLM answering 
   against retrieval-augmented inference.

3. We analyze model performance by question category (numerical reasoning, 
   conceptual understanding, information extraction), providing insights into 
   where different model families excel.

4. We release all evaluation code, datasets, and reproducibility instructions.

2. RELATED WORK
================================================================================

2.1 Financial Question Answering

The FinQA dataset (Chen et al., 2021) introduced the task of numerical reasoning 
over financial tables and text, requiring models to generate and execute 
multi-step calculation programs. The dataset demonstrated that even large models 
struggle with multi-step financial arithmetic. Subsequent work introduced 
ConvFinQA (Zheng et al., 2022) for multi-turn financial conversations.

FinanceBench (Islam et al., 2023) extended financial QA to cover document 
understanding over real SEC filings, testing whether models can accurately 
extract and reason about figures from actual company disclosures. The benchmark 
found that even frontier models like GPT-4 made significant errors, particularly 
on multi-hop questions.

FinTextQA (Chen et al., 2024) focused on long-form financial question answering 
over regulatory and textbook content, addressing a gap in existing benchmarks 
that focused primarily on short, factoid-style answers.

2.2 Open-Weight Language Models

The release of Llama (Touvron et al., 2023) and subsequently Llama 2 and 3 
(Touvron et al., 2023; Meta AI, 2024) established a strong baseline for 
open-weight model evaluation. Mistral 7B (Jiang et al., 2023) demonstrated 
that smaller models with efficient architectures (sliding window attention, 
grouped query attention) could match larger predecessors. Qwen2.5 (Alibaba, 
2024) introduced strong multilingual and mathematical capabilities. 
Phi-3 (Abdin et al., 2024) showed that aggressive data quality filtering 
enables small models to punch above their weight. DeepSeek-R1 (DeepSeek, 2025) 
introduced chain-of-thought distillation for reasoning tasks.

2.3 Retrieval-Augmented Generation

RAG (Lewis et al., 2020) demonstrated that combining parametric knowledge in 
LLM weights with non-parametric retrieval from an external corpus improves 
performance on knowledge-intensive tasks. For financial QA, RAG is particularly 
relevant because financial documents are long, regularly updated, and contain 
highly specific numerical information that may not be well captured in model 
training data. Prior work has applied RAG to financial QA (Yang et al., 2023) 
but systematic comparison across model families remains limited.

3. DATASETS
================================================================================

3.1 FinanceBench

FinanceBench (Islam et al., 2023) contains questions derived from real SEC 
10-K and 10-Q filings from publicly traded companies. The full benchmark 
contains 10,231 questions, but the publicly available portion consists of 
150 questions with associated evidence from financial documents. Questions 
span factoid retrieval, numerical calculation, and comparative reasoning 
across different reporting periods. We evaluate on the 150 publicly available 
questions and note that our results cannot be generalized to the full benchmark.

3.2 FinTextQA

FinTextQA (Chen et al., 2024) addresses long-form financial question answering. 
The publicly released portion covers policy and regulatory financial content. 
Approximately 240 question-answer pairs are available. The textbook portion 
described in the original paper is not publicly available. All results are 
therefore reported on the policy/regulation subset, and we document this 
limitation accordingly.

3.3 FinQA

FinQA (Chen et al., 2021) focuses on numerical reasoning over financial earnings 
reports. Each instance includes a table (from an earnings statement or similar 
document), surrounding text, a question, and a reference computation program 
specifying the arithmetic operations required to arrive at the correct answer. 
We use the official test split, which contains approximately 1,147 questions.

4. MODELS
================================================================================

We evaluate six open-weight large language models representing diverse model 
families, parameter counts, and training approaches:

(1) Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (Alibaba, 2024): A 7B-parameter instruction-tuned model 
from the Qwen 2.5 series, notable for strong mathematical and multilingual 
capabilities. Accessed via HF ID: Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct.

(2) Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (Meta AI, 2024): Meta's 8B instruction-following model 
from the Llama 3.1 release, featuring improved context length and instruction 
adherence. Requires license acceptance. HF ID: meta-llama/Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct.

(3) Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 (Mistral AI, 2024): A 7B model using sliding window 
attention and grouped query attention for efficient inference. HF ID: 
mistralai/Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3.

(4) Gemma-2-9B-it (Google, 2024): Google's 9B instruction-tuned Gemma 2 model. 
Requires license acceptance. HF ID: google/gemma-2-9b-it.

(5) Phi-3.5-mini-instruct (Microsoft, 2024): A 3.8B model trained on highly 
curated data, demonstrating strong benchmark performance relative to its size. 
HF ID: microsoft/Phi-3.5-mini-instruct.

(6) DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (DeepSeek, 2025): A 7B model distilled from 
DeepSeek-R1 with chain-of-thought reasoning capabilities. HF ID: 
deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B.

5. METHODOLOGY
================================================================================

5.1 Inference Setup

All six models are queried through the Hugging Face Inference Providers API 
using an OpenAI-compatible interface. This ensures consistent infrastructure 
across all models, preventing hardware or batching differences from introducing 
confounds. Generation parameters are identical for all models: temperature=0 
(greedy decoding) for reproducibility, maximum 512 new tokens.

5.2 Prompt Design

All models receive a consistent prompt structure:
- System message: "You are a financial analysis expert. Answer the question 
  based on the provided financial context. Be precise..."
- User message: Financial Context followed by the Question.

This structure follows standard instruction-following conventions compatible 
with all six models' chat templates.

5.3 Experiment A: Direct LLM Evaluation

In Experiment A, each model receives the question and the benchmark's provided 
context (the relevant passage or table from the financial document). This 
measures the model's ability to understand and use already-retrieved evidence.

5.4 Experiment B: RAG-Augmented Evaluation

In Experiment B, we replace the benchmark's provided context with retrieved 
context from our RAG pipeline. The corpus is built exclusively from document 
context fields across all three datasets; reference answers are never included 
in the retrieval corpus.

We use sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2 to generate 384-dimensional 
dense embeddings, indexed in FAISS with inner-product similarity. At query 
time, the top-5 chunks are retrieved and concatenated as the model's context. 
We also evaluate a BM25 sparse retrieval baseline using rank_bm25.

6. EVALUATION METRICS
================================================================================

We evaluate across five metric categories:

Answer Correctness:
• Exact Match (EM): 1 if normalized prediction equals normalized reference.
  Normalization: lowercase, punctuation removal, whitespace normalization.
• Token F1: Precision-recall F1 at the token level, as in SQuAD evaluation.
• ROUGE-L: Longest common subsequence F1 between prediction and reference.

Numerical Accuracy:
• Numeric Accuracy: Extracts numeric values from both prediction and reference,
  normalizes currency symbols, thousands separators, and percentage units, 
  and applies a 0.5% relative error tolerance.

Answer Quality:
• BERTScore: Contextual embedding-based precision, recall, and F1.
• Semantic Similarity: Cosine similarity between sentence-transformer embeddings.

Faithfulness:
• Lexical Faithfulness: Fraction of model's content tokens appearing in context.
  This is a proxy; NLI-based faithfulness would be more rigorous.
• Abstention Rate: Fraction of responses correctly indicating insufficient information.

Efficiency:
• Latency: Wall-clock inference time per question.
• Token Usage: Prompt and completion tokens per request.

9. LIMITATIONS
================================================================================

This work has several important limitations that readers should consider:

Dataset availability: We evaluate on publicly available portions of each 
benchmark only. FinanceBench provides 150 of 10,231 questions; FinTextQA 
provides ~240 of a larger described dataset; only FinQA's test split is fully 
available. Our results cannot be generalized to full benchmark performance.

Evaluation metric limitations: Exact Match is a strict metric that underestimates 
performance on paraphrased correct answers. Semantic similarity and BERTScore 
measure surface similarity, not factual correctness. Our faithfulness metric 
is a lexical proxy and cannot detect subtle factual inaccuracies.

Inference provider limitations: We used the Hugging Face Inference Providers 
serverless API. Provider-side optimizations (batching, quantization) may 
differ from paper-reported performance. Latency measurements reflect network 
conditions and queuing, not pure model inference time.

Evaluation scope: We evaluate models in a zero-shot setting. Few-shot or 
fine-tuned models would likely perform substantially better, particularly on 
FinQA's numerical reasoning programs.

Pilot evaluation: Default settings use a 5-question pilot sample. Full-scale 
results require running with PILOT_MODE=False and appropriate API quota.
